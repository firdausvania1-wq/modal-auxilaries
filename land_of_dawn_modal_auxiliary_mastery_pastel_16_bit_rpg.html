SLIDE 02 // FOUNDATION CODEX

Core Structural & Practical Rules

🔮

Core Definition

Modal auxiliaries modify the mood of the main verb to express possibility, necessity, permission, ability, or advice.

⚔️

Bare Infinitive Rule

Modals must be immediately followed by the base form of the verb without "to" (Modal+Verbbase), except for semi-modals that naturally include "to" (e.g., ought to, have to, be able to).

🛡️

Invariable Structure

Modals do not change form for different subjects; they never take third-person -s, past tense -ed, or continuous -ing endings.

🐉

No Auxiliary Stacking

You cannot combine two primary modal verbs directly (e.g., will can is incorrect; use will be able to instead).

SLIDE 12 // GRAND TREASURE TABLE

Overview Matrix: Practical Functions vs. Modals

Function Category Primary Modals Used Nuance / Strength Level
High Certainty / Deduction Must, Will Near 100% certainty based on facts or strong evidence
Moderate Probability Should, Ought to Expected to happen based on normal circumstances
Low Certainty / Possibility May, Might, Could Speculative, conditional, or tentative
Formal Permission May, Can May is formal/written; Can is informal/spoken
Prohibition Must not, Cannot Absolute restriction (Must not) vs lack of permission (Cannot)
Absence of Obligation Don't have to, Needn't Optional; no necessity exists
